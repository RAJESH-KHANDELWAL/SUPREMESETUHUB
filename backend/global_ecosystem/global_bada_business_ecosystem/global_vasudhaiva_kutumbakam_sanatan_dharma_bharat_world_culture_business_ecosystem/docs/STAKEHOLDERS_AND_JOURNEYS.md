# Stakeholders and journey design

## Stakeholder needs

| Stakeholder | Primary need | Trust requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Artisan / creator | Fair discovery, sales and attribution | Provenance, rights, fair fees and payment terms |
| Educator / researcher | Reach learners and collaborators | Accurate credentials, sources and content rights |
| Community organization | Control representation and benefit sharing | Consent, governance and complaint channels |
| Visitor / learner | Reliable information and clear terms | Accurate descriptions, safety and refunds |
| Buyer | Authentic product and dependable fulfillment | Origin, quality, price and return information |
| Service provider | Qualified inquiries and clear expectations | Fair onboarding and transparent ranking |
| Institution / partner | Reliable collaboration | Due diligence, written scope and accountability |
| Ecosystem operator | Safe, sustainable service delivery | Auditable decisions and defined responsibilities |

## Journey A — Creator or artisan onboarding
1. Creator submits profile and portfolio voluntarily.
2. Operator verifies identity and authority to represent the work.
3. Creator chooses public fields, attribution and permitted commercial uses.
4. Pricing, commission, fulfillment, payment timing and returns are agreed in writing.
5. Listing is reviewed for accuracy, safety and prohibited claims.
6. Creator can update, pause or request removal of their listing.
7. Sales, complaints and payment reconciliation are reviewed on a defined schedule.

## Journey B — Learner or visitor discovery
1. User selects interest, language, location and accessibility needs.
2. Discovery results clearly distinguish verified information from editorial suggestions.
3. User reviews provider credentials, program scope, price and terms.
4. Inquiry or booking is routed through an authorized channel.
5. Provider confirms availability and terms; no availability is implied by a directory listing.
6. User receives support and a clear cancellation/refund path.
7. Feedback is moderated and corrections are handled transparently.

## Journey C — Community partnership
1. Identify the community and its legitimate representatives without assuming a single spokesperson.
2. Explain purpose, data use, commercial intent, risks and possible benefits in accessible language.
3. Obtain documented, voluntary and appropriately scoped consent.
4. Agree decision rights, attribution, compensation, access restrictions and benefit sharing.
5. Conduct a small pilot with community oversight.
6. Review outcomes and complaints jointly.
7. Renew, revise or end the partnership according to the agreement.

## Journey D — Institution or sponsor collaboration
1. Verify legal identity, authority and reputation of the proposed partner.
2. Check conflicts, sanctions or other applicable restrictions through appropriate due diligence.
3. Define deliverables, budget, IP ownership, brand usage, data access and termination rights.
4. Obtain written approvals before public announcements.
5. Track deliverables, expenditures and outcome measures.
6. Publish only claims that are supported by the signed agreement and actual delivery.

## Service design requirements
- Plain-language descriptions and accessible contact routes.
- Clear distinction between directory listing, inquiry, confirmed booking and completed service.
- No fake reviews, undisclosed paid ranking or fabricated credentials.
- A way to report inaccuracies, misuse, cultural harm or safety concerns.
- Reasonable response-time expectations and escalation ownership.
- Localized language and culturally appropriate support where feasible.
