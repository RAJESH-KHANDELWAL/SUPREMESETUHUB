# Cultural ethics, consent and rights

## Representation
- Recognize that religious, regional, linguistic, caste, tribal, indigenous and other communities are internally diverse.
- Do not claim to represent a whole community based on one person's participation.
- Use community-preferred names and descriptions where reasonably ascertainable.
- Separate editorial interpretation from community testimony and documented historical evidence.
- Avoid stereotypes, exoticization, decontextualization and sensationalized sacred material.

## Consent protocol
Before collecting, recording, displaying, adapting, licensing or commercializing cultural material:
1. Identify the creator, custodian, community and any other relevant rights holders.
2. Explain the purpose, audience, channels, commercial intent, duration and possible risks.
3. Explain whether participation is voluntary and how consent can be withdrawn.
4. Agree attribution, compensation, access restrictions and permitted adaptations.
5. Record consent in an appropriate form and language.
6. Reconfirm consent if purpose, audience, commercial use or distribution changes.

Consent from an individual does not automatically grant rights held by a community, institution, employer, publisher or other creator.

## Rights and provenance register
For each relevant asset, record:
- Asset identifier, title/description and media type.
- Creator, contributor, custodian and source.
- Creation date or estimated period, if known.
- Copyright, trademark, design, privacy and other rights status.
- License terms, permitted channels, territory, duration and adaptations.
- Consent evidence and any community restrictions.
- Attribution wording and compensation/benefit-sharing agreement.
- Review date, expiry and withdrawal/contact route.

## Sacred, restricted and sensitive knowledge
Do not publish or commercialize material that is restricted, sacred, confidential or culturally sensitive without appropriate authority and permission. Respect seasonal, gendered, initiatory, location-based or other access restrictions when identified by the relevant community. When uncertain, pause publication and seek guidance rather than assuming public availability.

## Fair compensation and benefit sharing
- Negotiate terms before work begins.
- Explain gross price, deductions, commissions, taxes and payout timing.
- Pay contributors according to written terms.
- Consider collective/community benefit where community knowledge or resources are involved.
- Do not use unpaid exposure as a substitute for agreed compensation.
- Keep records of payments and agreed non-financial benefits.

## Correction, withdrawal and takedown
Provide a clear channel for rights holders and affected communities to request correction, restriction or removal. Acknowledge requests, assess urgency, preserve necessary evidence securely, pause disputed use where appropriate, and document the decision. Explain any legal or operational limitation transparently.
