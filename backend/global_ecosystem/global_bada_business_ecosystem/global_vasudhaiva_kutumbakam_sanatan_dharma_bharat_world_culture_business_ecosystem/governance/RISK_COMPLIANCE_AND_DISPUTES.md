# Risk, compliance and dispute management

## Risk register categories

| Risk | Example | Minimum control |
|---|---|---|
| Cultural harm | Misrepresentation or unauthorized sacred content | Community review, consent and takedown process |
| IP / rights | Unlicensed image, text, design or recording | Rights register and license review |
| Fraud / misrepresentation | False provider, origin or credential | Identity and claim verification |
| Consumer harm | Hidden fees, misleading refund terms | Plain-language terms and support route |
| Financial | Delayed payouts, chargebacks, unclear commissions | Reconciliation and documented payout policy |
| Safety | Unsafe event, travel or visitor experience | Risk assessment, qualified providers and emergency plan |
| Privacy | Excessive collection or public exposure | Data minimization, access control and retention policy |
| Cybersecurity | Account compromise or data leakage | Security controls owned by security/platform layers |
| Regulatory | Licensing, tax, consumer or cross-border noncompliance | Jurisdiction-specific professional review |
| Reputational | Unsupported endorsement or impact claims | Evidence review and correction process |
| Environmental | Visitor pressure or unsustainable sourcing | Capacity limits and sourcing checks |

## Compliance workstreams to assess
Applicable requirements depend on the countries, states, products and services involved. Obtain qualified advice on:
- Business registration, tax, invoicing and accounting.
- Consumer protection, advertising and e-commerce disclosures.
- Copyright, trademarks, design rights, image and performer releases.
- Privacy, data protection, cybersecurity and cross-border transfers.
- Tourism, transport, hospitality, food safety and event permits.
- Product standards, labeling, import/export and restricted products.
- Employment, contractor, volunteer and safeguarding requirements.
- Fundraising, grants, sponsorship and nonprofit rules where applicable.
- Sanctions, anti-bribery, conflicts of interest and partner due diligence where relevant.

This checklist is not legal advice and does not establish compliance.

## Dispute workflow
1. Receive and log the complaint with minimal necessary personal data.
2. Classify urgency: safety, rights/takedown, payment, quality, fraud or general grievance.
3. Acknowledge and provide a reference number and expected response time.
4. Preserve relevant records with restricted access.
5. Pause a listing, transaction or publication when continued activity could cause harm.
6. Gather both sides' evidence and apply published terms consistently.
7. Decide, explain the outcome and identify appeal/escalation options.
8. Record corrective action and monitor for recurrence.

## Incident response
For safety, privacy or security incidents, notify the accountable response owner immediately and follow the relevant legal notification duties. Do not publish incident details or affected people's information without authorization. Technical incident response is owned by the relevant security and platform teams.

## Launch-blocking conditions
Do not launch a relevant offering if rights are unclear, required permits are missing, safety controls are inadequate, fees are undisclosed, payout ownership is unclear, privacy controls are absent, or a credible community objection remains unresolved.
