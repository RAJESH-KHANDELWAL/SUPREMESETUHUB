# 🌍 GLOBAL VASUDHAIVA KUTUMBAKAM | SANATAN DHARMA | BHARAT SANSKRITI | WORLD CULTURE ECOSYSTEM

> **One World • Shared Humanity • Living Heritage • Cultural Wisdom • Respectful Exchange**

**A global cultural home for the wisdom, heritage, traditions, languages, arts, knowledge systems and living communities of Bharat and the world.**

This ecosystem is the cultural and knowledge identity layer: a place to understand, preserve, document, learn, experience and respectfully connect cultures. It is not merely a directory of folders. It represents a living vision in which cultural roots are understood in depth, traditions are represented with dignity, knowledge is carried forward, and people across regions and nations can discover one another without losing their distinct identities.

## ✨ The feeling and purpose

**Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam** expresses the ideal of the world as one family. This ecosystem turns that spirit into a practical cultural commitment: respect every community, learn across boundaries, preserve heritage with consent, and create opportunities for meaningful exchange.

**Sanatan Dharma** is approached through its many philosophical, spiritual, ethical, textual, artistic and living traditions. The ecosystem does not claim that one description or institution speaks for every school, lineage or community.

**Bharat Sanskriti** includes India's diverse civilizational memory, regional cultures, languages, literature, knowledge traditions, festivals, arts, crafts, architecture, foodways and contemporary creative expression.

**World Culture** means the many distinct cultures, peoples, languages, histories, arts, heritage sites and living practices around the globe. Exchange should build understanding, not erase differences.

## 🪷 Four cultural pillars

### 1. Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam — shared humanity
- Dialogue, mutual respect, compassion and cooperation across communities.
- Cultural exchange, intercultural learning and global friendship.
- Responsible community participation and shared public knowledge.

### 2. Sanatan Dharma — philosophical and living heritage
- Philosophical schools, texts, commentarial traditions and knowledge systems.
- Spiritual practices, festivals, temples, sacred geography and living traditions, represented with appropriate permissions.
- Sanskrit and other classical and contemporary knowledge/language traditions.
- Arts, music, dance, architecture, literature, yoga-related heritage and traditional learning, with context and evidence.

### 3. Bharat Sanskriti — India's plural heritage
- Regional and local cultures, languages, histories and community identities.
- Classical, folk, tribal, indigenous and contemporary arts and craft traditions.
- Literature, oral histories, manuscripts, architecture, archaeology and heritage.
- Festivals, food traditions, music, dance, performance, crafts and cultural institutions.

### 4. World Culture — global diversity
- Languages, histories, arts, heritage, creative communities and knowledge.
- Country, region and community-specific cultural contexts.
- Museums, archives, learning institutions and cultural exchange.
- Collaborative projects that respect local custodianship and rights.

## 🌱 What people should discover here

This cultural ecosystem is intended to help visitors, learners, researchers, cultural practitioners and communities discover:

- **Understand:** context, history, philosophies, cultural meanings and sources.
- **Explore:** places, heritage, art forms, languages, festivals and traditions.
- **Learn:** courses, books, research, workshops and language resources.
- **Preserve:** archives, oral histories, documentation and community-led projects.
- **Experience:** exhibitions, performances, responsible visits and cultural programs.

## 🛡️ Respect, evidence and community safeguards

- Preserve cultural, regional, linguistic, philosophical and community distinctions.
- Distinguish documented history, academic interpretation, oral history, traditional belief and personal opinion.
- Cite sources, preserve provenance and record uncertainty rather than presenting disputed claims as settled facts.
- Seek informed permission before recording, publishing, adapting or commercially using community material.
- Respect restricted, sacred, private and sensitive knowledge; do not assume that online availability means permission for reuse.
- Use community-preferred descriptions and avoid stereotypes, tokenism or claims of speaking for an entire community.
- Correct inaccurate material and provide a clear route for concerns, attribution corrections and takedown requests.
- Do not imply government, religious institution, community or academic endorsement without documented authorization.
